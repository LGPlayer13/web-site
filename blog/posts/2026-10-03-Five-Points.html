definitely not a waste of four months

took me a bit longer than a month, huh? i would've written something sooner, but i've been working on this damn version a lot longer than anticipated. my design philosophy has changed quite a lot these past few months. the whole "liquid glass" thing i had going was never going to work out, to say the least. version 4.1 (beta) might've actually been the ugliest thing i've created since the v2.0 days. i mean, seriously, did you see those blog post pages? yuck. cramming two-thousand word posts into a tiny ass box was not my greatest move ever.

okay... i'm ragging on myself too much. neocities websites always go through an odd design or two. just look at me, i'm on what, version 5.0 now?
luckily for you, this version does not contain a stupid "(beta)" tag! it's essentially feature-complete! with this release, you don't have to continuously scroll through a dumbass box to read this. instead, you've been given the chance to gawk at the lack of a proper navbar. wow, how cool is that?!

core design aside, i can tell that i've actually enjoyed making v5.0 over v4.1. even if development has been painfully slow, it's been a blast to make. no longer shall i be held back by poor CSS design, outdated classes, or overly complex grids! though, sometimes i miss those times where v3.0 took just six whole days.

me a few months ago never would've styled a blog post like this. but i don't care for that guy anymore. we gotta press on, like with most things in life. although, i did make an Archives page for documenting the most important revisions and events of the site. it's kinda like the Patch Notes page, but a lot more focused. speaking of the Patch Notes page, you can type in the terminal now. you're welcome, hyphen!
oh yeah, i kiiiiinda put aside my games to make this whole site update happen, heh. to be honest, i don't know when any new game is gonna release. i just keep getting busier and busier. it's been five years since George 2 began development, and it doesn't look like that number is getting any lower.

i'll try to get SOMETHING done. that new mining game i put in the to-do list, that dating sim i talked about a few months back, i dunno. it's probably gonna be the mining game, at least if i don't come back to the Remastered Collection update beforehand. or, y'know, my lazy bum could just do both.

LG's five points

so, as you may know, v5.0 was basically made on a whim. v4.1 wasn't working out. at all. the homepage was by far the easier part, and it was the only page that actually turned out fairly decent. there may have not been a whole lot to look at, but it introduced the status thought bubble, which i really like.

i then moved on to the blog post pages, which i had never really gotten right. the text was always either too hard to read or, as introduced by my new design, crunched into a dumbass box. it's clear now that i still hadn't gotten the pages right. what i DID get right, however, was adding sections to each blog post. i don't know how i hadn't thought of this beforehand. not only do sections make each post organized and focused, but they also give me an excuse to come up with lame one-liners.

then it all came crashing down. the main blog page was up next, and i struggled day after day with this one. not only was i trying to design a good-looking blog, but i was also making the core design along with it! how on earth could i design a completely new page without having the fundamentals to guide me? as it turns out, i couldn't, at least for the next month.

eventually i landed on a "working" design, but the hard truth had hit me like a stack of bricks. i had just spent a MONTH on a SINGLE page. now imagine how long it would take me to make the damn About page. i'd probably be at version 6.0 by then.
i started to rethink my entire design philosophy. i may have gotten away with it for the past few months, but the site wasn't gonna survive at this rate. what i needed was a complete refresh. this "minimalist liquid glass" schtick had to go.

this time around, i was going for a more "flat" style. no more fancy box shadows or borders, we're sticking to a simple palette and consistent design. i even busted out my trusty Algebra II notebook to sketch some new layouts, which i'd made a (good) habit of doing since late last year. and within a few days, i had a complete homepage! it looked clean, was much more legible, and came with a proper space for the latest announcement.

the blog and blog post pages went just about as smooth. i even threw in the Old Blog pages just for the hell of it, even if they required a substantial amount of clean up. soon after, i remembered that i hadn't actually written a blog post in, what, two months?! right, i ditched the monthly schedule when i wrote my last blog post. well, better late than never, am i right?

after that, the pages slowly started to roll in. new ones even joined in on the fun as well! we got a kickass Art Gallery going, an brand new Museum filled with useless trivia, and a character galler- oh. i didn't actually make that.
so you remember how i said that this update was essentially feature complete? well, it was a taaaad rushed. i planned on there being around 34 pages complete for the update's release. however, development was so goddamn slow that i ended up cutting out some stuff. individual game pages? nope. character gallery? postponed. the shrine? also postponed.
...okay, so that list was a bit smaller than anticipated (and not even completely true). but hey, there's no denying that this update is still jam-packed with goodies.

so, with that whole recap done, that brings us to now. a monumental update for LG product fans, and a new post talking about said update! god, i've missed writing. problem is, i'm still too depressed as balls to write general life updates. guess a devlog will have to do! no trigger warnings today, hah!

curse that darned Copilot key

now that i've given you a proper deep dive into my new design philosophy, i'd like to talk about IDEs. if you don't know what those are, don't worry! i had to look it up right before writing this. simply put, IDEs are what techbros use to vibecode their shitty dev portfolios. however, they can also be used by (slightly more) normal people to make life a whole lot easier.

back when i was first writing my first bit of HTML, i used Neocities' built-in text editor. and, well, it was total garbage! it was slow, inefficient, and lacked the bells and whistles of competent text editors. fast forward a few months, and i learned about a little piece of software called Brackets. now Brackets was like VSCode, but specifically tailored for web development. and it was very useful! once i got it set up alongside GitHub Desktop, i was on a roll.

a few web site versions later, i learned about a new piece of software called Phoenix Code. now Phoenix Code was basically the exact same as Brackets, only with a new logo and better live preview. and it was a tad more useful! i may have not had the best setup for expert web development, but i was quite comfortable. nothing could ever ruin this perfect moment.

knock knock oh, who is it? an update to Phoenix Code? well, don't mind if i- oh. that's... not good, actually. say hello to a data center's best friend, AI! if there's one thing to add to a text editor, it's a horrible, piece of shit AI that literally no one fucking asked for!!

the geniuses at Phoenix Code HQ decided to inject a bunch of AI garbage into their perfectly fine editor, complete with a big ass "AI" button in the left-hand corner of the screen. you were also automatically given a free trial of the Phoenix Pro plan, so you had access to all the lovely AI features. okay, but like, did anyone actually ASK for this? you could already add extensions to the editor, so why not just... put the AI there??? instead, Phoenix Code pivoted from an editor "made to be creative" to an editor made for "staying creative with AI on your side".

obviously, you're not REQUIRED to use the AI. but the fact that they're pushing it so heavily and completely abandoning their morals just rubs me the wrong way. for an experienced team of coders with the goal to "place the human aspect of coding at the heart of the user experience", they've done a shit job at keeping that legacy going.

aside from the AI junk, they also made the editor just a lot more of a pain in the ass to use. constantly asking you to make an account, not having a proper settings menu (or a way to even hide the AI stuff), and just generally being annoying. so i looked for alternatives. i thought about using Kate, but then i looked to VSCode. i already used it for making IGM games, so why not make it my go-to IDE? and that's what i did.

so in the end, the whole Phoenix Code AI situation only ended up being a minor inconvenience for me. i've found out that VSCode is actually quite nice, and it even comes with built-in support (which Phoenix Code also has, but you have to go through a bunch of bs to activate it).
