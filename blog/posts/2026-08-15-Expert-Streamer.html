turns out, streaming is harder than it looks. it's especially difficult when you can't even use your phone to see the damn stream chat!! useless ass youtube studio app...
what about making videos? y'know, the entire point of Youtube in the first place? i've actually done quite a bit of that as well! granted, i haven't made anything truly outstanding, nor have i made anything really "passable" for that matter. my videos were unpolished, unedited, unfiltered (unfortunately), and were plagued with volume issues. my channel was in a state of decay, and it didn't seem like that would be changing any time soon.

you already know where this is going. summer came around, i was bored; cue channel-improving montage.

getting a good look at my channel, i realized how much clean up i had in store for me. everything was all out of wack: my profile picture and banner were outdated, my bio was bland, my thumbnails were ugly, and i didn't even know how to properly edit a video. didn't help with the fact that i had no idea what to even make a video about. so the first order of business was brainstorming.

a little while later, someone gave me the idea of making a video on Chirpy 4, a Scratch game from 2019. i mean, of course i'd make a video on one of my personal favorite games (on Scratch). so i recorded an hour of footage, opened DaVinci Resolve, and... came to a screeching halt. "no, this won't do," i thought to myself. not only did i still lack video editing expertise, but the thing wouldn't even import the damn footage properly! so now i was at a bit of a dead end. turns out, on Linux, you need the Studio (paid) version of DR to import .mp4 files. why? idk

luckily, i had an ace up my sleeve: a lovely piece of video editing software called Kdenlive. and boy, was it a big help.

opening up Kdenlive for the first time was a LOT less daunting than DaVinci. plus, it didn't need an outdated, hyper-specific version to run properly! the program was smooth as butter (usually), importing video/audio was a breeze, and it was just generally responsive and felt good to use. it is a bit of a memory hog, though. but i can live with that, at least until it eats up 20 gigs of RAM.

my first Chirpy 4 video was the first time i actually bothered with effects. zoom-ins, moving elements, fade-ins, clips, you name it. my favorite bit from that video was the series of jabs at Chirpy 3, complete with a "no Chirpy 3" graphic and loud buzzer sound. ah, that game is such a stinking pile of shit.
i actually had a ton of fun making that video, and people commented on that. its sequel was also a blast to make, too, complete with a proper intro and outro sequence advertising my mod for the game.

soon after making those videos, i took some time to give my channel a proper makeover, since i hadn't touched it in over a year at that point. i gave it a new banner, new description, new playlists, and even made some new thumbnails for the few passable videos i have. it wasn't anything out of this world, but it was a breath of fresh air for my once-neglected channel. working on those two videos just brought a smile to my face. it's fun to make stuff, y'know? videos i can actually look back on with joy and whimsy, rather than saying "guess i had nothin' better to do". i had plans to make another video before the end of summer, but, as always, life gets in the way.

i hate it when life does that
